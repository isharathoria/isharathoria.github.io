How to actually build this

01Go-to-market — sequencing, not everything at once

Tap a phase to expand.

Pick one activity/sport wedge and commit to it publicly. Rather than starting a community from scratch, embed in the ones that already exist — run clubs, gym crews, WhatsApp/Instagram fitness groups — for several months, and let them spec the product (sizing, pockets, fabric feel) directly. Run fit/pattern R&D against real Indian-body measurements in parallel. Small, measured paid spend begins from month 3, alongside the community work.

Launch with 4 SKUs — 2 sports bras, 1 short compression tights, 1 shorts — enough range to cover the core use cases without diluting fit depth on any one. Sell direct via your own site + Instagram, and add marketplace presence (Myntra, Nykaa Fashion) within the first 9 months rather than waiting for later validation. Keep COD low and prepaid-favoring to protect margin (COD returns run 25–42% vs. 8–15% prepaid). Get the product onto real athletes at the events your customer actually cares about — this is the "proof event," documented unfiltered, flaws included.

Expand SKUs within the same category (don't jump sports or genders yet). Deepen marketplace presence and reorder volume as repeat-purchase and return-rate data comes in. First physical touchpoints — pop-ups, partner gyms/studios. Paid performance marketing scales meaningfully here, once CM2 is positive or clearly trending there — small measured tests already began in Phase 0.

Only once the first wedge is profitable does a second activity or the second gender make sense — this is when Gymshark added women's (years after launching men's-only) and when Lululemon moved beyond yoga. Retail stores and a genuine ₹4–5K premium tier belong here too.

02How performance is actually engineered — and what's copyable

"Why do Nike/UA/Gymshark perform well" has a specific, unglamorous answer: fiber-level engineering, knit/weave construction, and seam placement — in that order. None of it is a secret formula, and most of it is buildable at Tiruppur scale.

Nike — Dri-FIT
Capillary action

Fine synthetic (mostly polyester) fibers form micro-channels that pull sweat outward by surface tension, spreading it across a larger area to evaporate faster. It's a yarn/fiber property, not a Nike-exclusive chemical — the same principle is available from any qualified technical-yarn mill.

Under Armour — HeatGear
4-way stretch + flatlock

Sweat-wicking performance fibers, 4-way stretch construction for multi-directional movement, and ergonomic flatlock seams to cut chafing. HeatGear Elite adds NEOLAST™, a spandex-free stretch fiber — the rest is standard technical-apparel construction.

Gymshark — Seamless
Warp-knit machinery

Polyester/nylon/spandex blends plus mesh ventilation zones, moving toward warp-knit seamless construction that eliminates traditional seams entirely. This one genuinely needs specialized machinery — see the stitching tiers below.

Replicable at Tiruppur scale, now

Technical polyester/nylon/spandex yarn with moisture-wicking finish (sourced via Canton Fair or Indian mills) · four-way stretch knits · flatlock seams for chafe reduction · mesh ventilation panels at sweat zones · compression mapping through pattern-cutting, not fabric chemistry.

Yet to be tested if it can be done

Full seamless warp-knit construction (Gymshark's newer lines) · proprietary fiber chemistry with years of in-house R&D · bonded/ultrasonic seams at low MOQ (limited Indian suppliers today) — these are Phase 2+ upgrades, not Day 1 requirements.

Where your differentiation actually comes from

Not from inventing a fiber Nike hasn't already engineered — that's decades of R&D you can't out-spend. Your edge is applying the same construction rigor (flatlock seams, ventilation paneling, compression mapping) to a pattern calibrated for Indian body proportions specifically — none of the three global brands do this. The differentiation has to be real performance function — fit accuracy, chafe reduction, compression placement — not an aesthetics-only story dressed up as technical. Athflex already uses "for Indian bodies" as a tagline, so the positioning itself isn't unclaimed — the gap is proving it works in training, not just in marketing copy. Two SKUs engineered this way out-differentiate a wider line built on generic aesthetics.

04Sourcing & manufacturing

Tap a card to expand.

The Canton Fair runs three phases a year; textiles and fashion sit in Phase 3 (autumn 2026: Oct 31–Nov 4). It recently added a dedicated functional & technical textiles zone — waterproof, anti-bacterial, moisture-wicking fabric, direct from mills, where you can compare GSM, composition, and finishing quality in person.

The International Pavilion is where English communication is easiest, and it also hosts Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, and Turkish suppliers alongside the Chinese ones — useful for benchmarking a Chinese quote against a regional alternative in the same hall.

Practical use for a first collection: go to source technical yarn/fabric rolls, not finished garments — then get cut-and-sew done domestically. This "Chinese yarn, Indian stitching" hybrid is close to what several credible Indian performance brands already run quietly.

Canton Fair vs. "normal" sourcing: the fair isn't a supply chain, it's a discovery event — twice a year, you meet dozens of mills face-to-face, compare GSM/composition/finishing side by side, and negotiate first terms with people whose factory you can (in principle) walk into. Ongoing sourcing after that first trip is different: it runs through direct contact with the mills you've already vetted (email, WeChat, or a sourcing agent), or through digital B2B platforms like Alibaba/1688 for smaller reorders — faster and cheaper per touch, but riskier if you haven't physically vetted the supplier first. The realistic pattern for a first collection: use the Fair (or a sourcing agent attending on your behalf) to vet and shortlist 2–3 mills once, then move to direct or digital contact for every reorder after that.

Importing fabric/garments from China into India stacks: Basic Customs Duty 20–25% ad valorem (or ₹100–500 per unit, whichever is higher), plus a 10% Social Welfare Surcharge on the BCD, plus IGST of 5–12% on the total value including duty and surcharge. Specific textile categories can additionally attract anti-dumping duty.

Net effect: a directly-imported finished technical fabric roll typically lands ~35–45% above its ex-factory quote once everything stacks — which is why "source yarn/grey fabric, finish and cut-sew in India" tends to beat "import finished fabric outright" on landed cost.

Tiruppur-based activewear manufacturers typically offer: flatlock and overlock stitching, in-house sourcing of lycra blends, recycled nylon, poly-spandex and technical mesh, an accessible MOQ of 50 pieces per style in mixed sizes, 2–3 week fit-sample turnaround, 4–6 week bulk production, and a standard 30/40/30 milestone payment structure.

This is a realistic, low-MOQ entry point for a first collection — the bonded/seamless tier below is a later-stage upgrade, not a Day 1 requirement.

Tier 1 · Standard

Overlock

The default stretch-seam finish — durable, widely available, slightly bulky.

Tier 1 · Activewear standard

Flatlock

Flat, low-bulk seam that lies against the skin — reduces chafing vs. overlock. Widely available in Tiruppur at low MOQ.

Tier 2 · Premium

Bonded / ultrasonic-welded

No thread — heat/ultrasonic-fused for a fully smooth look. Fewer Indian manufacturers have the machines at low MOQ — a later-stage upgrade.

Tier 3 · Highest-end

Seamless knitting

Garment knitted in one continuous piece, zero side seams. Machinery is overwhelmingly China-based — not a Month-1 choice.
